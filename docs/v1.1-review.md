# Version 1.1 - review against the six questions

Asked on 17 September 2026: is this thing actually doing its job? Six questions, answered from the
code and from the real bundles in hand, not from intention. Where the answer was no, it is fixed
in this version.

Short version: **two of the six were genuinely broken, and one of those had already cost us a
case.**

---

## 1. Does it collect files in minimal steps?

**Mostly yes, with one silly gap - fixed.**

Watch folders are the right design and cost nothing once set up. But a bundle arriving by email
meant: save it, find the watched folder, save it there again, wait for the watcher. Four steps to
do the one thing the app exists for.

**v1.1:** drag bundles onto the window, or use **Import files...**. Both go through the same
processor, so nothing behaves differently from a watched folder.

One deliberate difference: an imported file skips the "newer than N days" filter. A watched folder
needs that filter or re-copying an archive would flood the list. A file somebody dragged in has
already been chosen, and ignoring it on a technicality would be the worst possible answer.

---

## 2. Is the analysis fair, or does it lean on what might be?

**It was not fair. Three separate problems, all fixed.**

**It called operator actions faults.** The fault-word list included `stop`, `stopped` and `press`,
so "Stop All Pressed" was a fault. On the real M22215 export that is 29 occurrences, and it headed
the DOES IT REPEAT section.

**It then asserted a cause, unconditionally.** Under that list the report printed *"A fault
repeating at the same step across attempts points at real hardware or a sensor, not a glitch"* -
every time, whatever had repeated. So the report sent a technician to meter a sensor for an
operator pressing a stop button.

**It treated the machine's own habits as faults.** "Outfeed clamps not used to prevent jamb"
(100 occurrences) matched on the word `jam`. "Unsafe to Move Axis" fires every time the blade moves
during a cut - 370 times in that one export.

**v1.1:**

- Operator actions are recognised and reported in their own right, never as faults. They are still
  worth knowing: somebody pressing stop 29 times in a session says something real about how the
  machine was behaving, which is a different question from what is broken.
- The causal sentence is printed only when the fault really did land on the same step every time,
  and the wording is hedged to "worth treating as real" rather than "points at".
- **Anything appearing in more than half of a machine's attempts is treated as that machine's
  normal behaviour, not a fault** - and the report says in its notes that it made that call, so it
  can be argued with. This is a frequency rule rather than a list of words on purpose: the next
  machine will have its own habits and nobody will remember to add them.
- What to check now depends on what failed. A servo movement error points at the drive, the
  coupling and anything fouling the axis, not at a sensor.
- A repeat that lands on a different step each time now says so, instead of picking the first one
  and printing a step number the rest of the report contradicts.

On the M22215 export the fault list went from **202 attempts "with a fault"** to **4** - and what
is left is the thing that actually mattered.

---

## 3. Does it use the operator's own words, and the timing?

**Both were thin. Both improved.**

**The words.** "manual to 335 thntd, no action" matched no complaint topic at all, so the report
said *"Nothing in that matches a complaint we have a routine for"* and worked from the logs alone.
THNTD is a word this codebase already knew - `TwoHandControlCheck` reads it - and the complaint
router had never heard of it.

**v1.1** adds two topics: *pressed the two-hand control and nothing happened* (thntd, two hand, no
action, nothing happened, no cut, won't go) and *driving the machine by hand from the HMI* (manual,
by hand, jog). That complaint now routes to both.

**The timing.** The report leaned on "the file is exported within minutes of the problem" without
ever checking. It is checkable.

**v1.1** prints how long after the last log line the export was taken. On the M22215 bundle:
**57.5 seconds**. That turns an assumption into a fact, and where the gap is large it says so
instead.

Beyond six hours it reports the gap as not knowable rather than inventing a number: the machine's
clock and the PC's are two different clocks, and the bundle timestamp is in UTC while the log is in
machine local time.

> Worth knowing: the file-name timestamp really is UTC. The M22215 bundle is named
> `9_16_2026 9-01-21 PM` and its own files were written at `2026-09-17 09:01:20` - exactly UTC+12.
> The `FileNameTimesAreUtc` setting being on by default is correct, at least for NZ machines. If a
> bundle ever turns up from a machine that writes local time, that setting is where to look.

## 4. Does it establish normal before scrutinising the end?

**Only narrowly, and not where you could see it.**

`StepOutcomeCheck` compared the last step against its usual next step and typical dwell, which is
genuinely good, but it is one step and it sat well down the report. Nothing established what a
normal *attempt* looks like, so every report read as though the last few minutes were remarkable.
Sometimes they are not.

**v1.1** adds **WHAT NORMAL LOOKS LIKE HERE**, placed *before* anything is said about the end of
the log: how many attempts completed, what a typical one took, the spread, how many carried a
fault, and how the last attempt compares.

On M22215: *224 of 355 completed, a typical one took 3.4 s, most between 3.1 s and 3.6 s. The last
attempt took 3.9 min - far longer than this machine's own habit. It did not complete.*

Where fewer than three attempts completed it says there is no yardstick, rather than quoting an
average of two things.

Everything in it is this machine against itself, on this day. Not against another machine and not
against a specification.

---

## 5. Does it show enough raw, uninterrupted log?

**No. This was the worst defect in the product, and it had already cost us a case.**

"HOW IT ENDED" showed *"the last 12 lines that say something"*. Those lines were filtered three
ways, and the first filter kept only `Other` and `MotionEvent` categories. **Every InputChange and
OutputChange line was dropped.**

On the M22215 export, six of the last twenty lines are InputChange, and all six were removed -
including this one:

```
09:00:13.819  InputChange   LidOpenRequest   Changed to 1     <- dropped
09:00:13.990  MotionEvent   Node0 Status     Servo Disabled   <- shown
```

The report showed the servo disabling with the thing that caused it, 0.17 s earlier, taken out.
Nobody reading it could tell why the machine stopped, and the support person who raised the case
wrote that the servos disabling was an unexplained second symptom. It was not. It was the lid
interlock doing its job.

**v1.1** prints **the last 50 lines of MachineLog.txt exactly as written** - every line, every
category, in file order, with line numbers - as its own section at the end of the report. The
summary is still there and still useful. It is no longer there *instead of* the file.

---

## 6. Any bugs needing attention?

**One serious, found by looking rather than by luck.**

**The fault scan could not see MotionEvent at all.** `FindFaults` began
`if (entry.Category != MachineLogCategory.Other) continue;`, and MotionEvent is where the
drive-level faults live. Across the sample logs that hid:

| Hidden fault | Occurrences |
|---|---|
| `Servo Movement Error` | 4 |
| `Servo Not Setup` | 2 |
| `Needs to be Homed` | 8 |
| `Needs to be Reset` | 1 |

Only Omron F-codes were being picked up, by a different check with its own code list.

**On the M22215 case, two of those four Servo Movement Errors happened inside the window the
operator was complaining about - at 08:57:02 on both nodes - and the report never mentioned one.**
It is now the headline of that report's last attempt.

Other things checked and found sound: no unguarded `First()`/`Single()`, no integer-division
mistakes in the statistics, no swallowed exceptions, no obvious division by zero.

Two smaller ones fixed in passing: a repeating fault listing 200 attempt numbers in one
unreadable line, and the same fault being quoted at two different steps in two sections of the same
report.

---

## What is still not right

- **The habit rule has not yet been exercised on real data**, because the two known habits are also
  caught by name. It is tested, and it is the general safety net, but the first machine it
  actually saves us on will be its real trial.
- **`Needs to be Homed` may be routine at startup** on some machines rather than a fault. Eight
  occurrences across every sample, so there is not enough to tell. If it starts crying wolf, the
  frequency rule should catch it - watch for it.
- **The baseline uses completed attempts only.** On a machine having a bad day that may be a small
  and unrepresentative sample, and the report says so rather than working around it.
- **Timing rests on the PC and the machine agreeing about what time it is.** For a NZ support
  person looking at a NZ machine that holds. For an Australian bundle it does not, and the report
  will report the gap as not knowable rather than getting it wrong.
